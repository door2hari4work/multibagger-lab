# Interpretation

## Position sizing (section 2)
- Diversification helps more than any sizing trick: equal top25 gives MDD -31.5% (-Rs 37,24,947 on a bigger base), CAGR 26.7%, Calmar 0.85 vs base 0.71; top40 -27.8% / 23.4%. top10 has the highest CAGR (30.7%) but worst month -18.7% and drifted to a 21% single position. CAGR is not monotone in top_n (10: 30.7, 15: 24.5, 25: 26.7), which is noise from one path, so do not read the top-15 vs top-25 difference as an effect.
- Inverse-vol weighting did nothing for MDD at top15 (-34.9%) and a little at top25 (-30.9% vs -31.5%). Not worth the complexity.
- Max-weight caps and vol-targeting reduce MDD only by holding cash: top15 cap5% gives MDD -26.9% but CAGR 18.4% (avg 55% invested); vol-target 15% gives -24.8% / 16.5%. The Calmar ratio does not improve (0.67-0.79 vs 0.71), i.e. you buy shallower drawdowns at roughly proportional cost in return. Weights drift between monthly rebalances (an equal 1/15 slot reached 14.6% of NAV in June 2010 and 21% for top10), and caps are applied only at rebalance. No sector data, so no sector cap was tested.

## Stops and the regime filter (sections 3-4 and regime_years.py)
- **The 30% trailing stop does not cut the tail here; it costs return and slightly worsens MDD.** With regime on: no stop MDD -32.9% / CAGR 25.6%; stop 30% -34.4% / 24.5%; stop 20% -34.6% / 22.5%; stop 15% -35.6% / 20.4% (265 stops). Tighter is monotonically worse. The worst single trade is -48.7% without a stop and -47.0% with it; 5th-percentile trade -21.0% vs -21.4%. The momentum/52-week-high entry filters and monthly rebalance already drop most deteriorating names, so the stop adds little.
- Whipsaw evidence: after a 30% stop, 53% of the sold names were higher 63 trading days later and 47% were more than 10% higher (mean +6.8%; +19.6% after 126 days). At 15-20% stops, 58-61% were higher after 63 days. The stopped capital then sits in cash (the engine re-enters only at the next month-end).
- Gap risk: the stop is on a close-to-close basis (trigger at close, fill at next close). Of 33 stops at 30%, 64% filled below the stop level; median shortfall -1.5%, mean -2.9%, 10th percentile -15.0%, worst -24.3% (PGEL 2013-02-28: trade return -47.0%; KIRLOSENG 2010-12-27 -20.6% below level; WOCKPHARMA 2013-05-24 -16.8%). So a "30% stop" realised -47% in the worst case. Close-only data cannot show true intraday/overnight gaps or circuit-locked exits; in real life stop orders on illiquid mid/small caps can be worse. Daily drops while held: 55 of 23,957 holding-days below -10% (0.23%), 3 below -20%, worst -39.0% in one day.
- **The regime filter (Nifty > 200-dma) does not cut the tail that mattered.** It kept the strategy in cash 26.6% of days (avg exposure 72.8%), but in 2018 it was 100% invested at both the peak and the trough, because Nifty stayed above its 200-dma while the survivor mid/small-cap book fell 34% (worst month 2018-09). MDD regime on vs off: -32.9% vs -32.8% (no stop), -34.4% vs -33.9% (30% stop). It helped in 2016 (-5.7% vs -16.2%, avoiding the post-2015 / demonetisation weakness) but cost 2012 (+21.8% vs +48.4%), 2013, 2015 and 2017, and even 2011 (-10.3% vs -8.0%; the cash stretch Jun-2011 to Feb-2012 would have earned +14.3% in these survivors). CAGR 24.5% vs 30.5% (stop 30%) and 25.6% vs 32.5% (no stop); Calmar 0.71 vs 0.90 and 0.78 vs 0.99. Longest underwater was shorter without the filter (441-609 d vs 727 d). On this data the filter is a return tax with no demonstrated drawdown benefit; this could reverse in a market with a broad index crash (2008, 2020 are outside the window).
- Cost of cutting risk in Rs terms (Rs 10,00,000 start, final value): no stop/no regime ends at Rs 1,25,82,371 (CAGR 32.5%, MDD -32.8% = -Rs 60,10,204 on a larger base) vs base Rs 71,78,036.

## Single-stock ruin (section 5)
- At equal weights the largest position is 1/15 = 6.67%: **Rs 66,667 on Rs 10,00,000 at the start, Rs 7,05,430 at the Rs 1,05,81,457 peak NAV**. If that name went to zero at the 2018 trough on top of the observed drawdown, NAV would be about Rs 64,41,245, which is -39.1% from peak (vs -34.4%). With drift the largest position was up to 14.6% of NAV (June 2010), and Rs 9,81,803 in absolute terms (2018-01-08, 9.4% of NAV).
- Top10: 10% each (Rs 1,00,000 at start; drifted to 21%); top25: 4% each (Rs 40,000 at start).
- Empirical worst single-position trade in the survivor-only data was -47.0%, with no trade below -50%, which is exactly what survivorship bias produces: names that went to zero are not in the sample. A stop would not protect against suspension/delisting (no exit possible). Illustrative only (assumed, not measured): if a held name has a 1% yearly chance of going to zero, the CAGR drag is about 1 pp and one wipeout is expected about every 6.7 years on 15 names; at 3% the drag is about 3 pp. A real Nifty 500 point-in-time universe over 2010-2018 contained far more distressed names than this sample, so the true probability is probably not small.

# Max-loss budget recommendation
What the user must be able to stomach, for the base rule (all scaled to Rs 10,00,000 invested):
1. **Observed in 2010-2018 (best case, survivor data): -34% = about Rs 3,44,000 down from the peak**, over 267 days (Jan-Oct 2018), with no recovery by the window end. (Rs 36,42,242 down from the Rs 1.06 cr peak if the account had compounded.) Intra-year, the worst month was -15.8% (Rs 1,58,000 on Rs 10 L) and a bad calendar year -22.9%.
2. **Planning budget: -50% (Rs 5,00,000 per Rs 10,00,000)**, plus allow for one extra single-name zero at -6.7% (Rs 66,667), i.e. roughly -55% as the number the user should be able to sit through without selling. Basis: 1.5x the observed MDD (judgement, not a model), because the data excludes 2008-type and 2020-type crashes, delisted names and gap-through stops (realised stop losses were up to -47% for a "30%" stop), and random picks in the same engine drew down up to -45.7%. If the user cannot tolerate -50%, size the strategy down to that fraction of the portfolio rather than tightening stops.
3. **Time budget: plan for 2-3 years underwater** (observed 727 d from 2010-11, 426 d from 2015-08, and an open stretch from 2018-01; Nifty itself took 1088 d to recover in 2010-13).
4. Rolling-entry view: a user entering on a random day in 2010-2018 had a median worst later loss of -4.7% from entry, 10% of entry days saw -18.6% or worse, 6.8% of entry days dipped >20% below entry value, and the worst entry (2018-01) -34.4%. (Nifty median -8.0%; the strategy compounds faster so it escapes quickly in good paths.)
5. Rule-set implications for the freeze: prefer top_n >= 25 equal weight (MDD -31.5%, Calmar 0.85); the 30% stop and the regime filter do not demonstrably reduce drawdown here and cost CAGR, so keep them only if the test criteria are checked with them on and off. Neither was tuned on result quality here; this is diagnostics only.

## Caveats
- Survivorship: biggest bias, all numbers optimistic. ^NSEI price index, so no dividends. Single 9-year path with two big years (2014 +119%, 2017 +115%) driving the CAGR; Nifty/EW comparisons use the same dates.
- Random baseline here is 10 unfiltered seeds, not the protocol's 1000 runs.
- Stops fill at next close, not intraday; gap and liquidity risk are understated. Costs are a flat 25 bps; no ADV or circuit check; fractional shares.
- Drawdown not recovered by the window end: the MDD may deepen out of sample (2019-2020 not looked at, by design).
