# Smallcase decision memo (2026-10-09). Research support, not personalised advice; the investor decides.

Inputs from the investor: Rs 50,000 to Rs 2 lakh one-time; monthly SIP optional, Rs 5-10k comfortable, Rs 15k maximum; a 20-25% fall is the limit; wants to see all options before choosing.
Sources: reports/smallcase/01..05 (every number there carries its source and date; items marked UNVERIFIED stay unverified here).

## What a smallcase is
Basket of direct stocks bought into the investor's own demat through a broker. A SEBI-registered research analyst or adviser (the manager) publishes the basket; rebalances are never automatic and every sell is taxable.

## Findings that decide the choice
1. At Rs 50k-2L, paid baskets are uneconomic. Subscriptions run Rs 7,000-13,000 a year, i.e. 4-13% of Rs 1-2 lakh before any return. Only free baskets (Windmill Capital's trackers) are sensible at this size.
2. The free trackers are cheap sector exposure, not alpha: over 2 years most are flat to negative; their 3-year numbers come from the 2023-24 rally; since 2019 Banking (8.1%) and Rural Demand (7.2%) trail the Nifty 500 (11.8%).
3. "Conviction in the manager" cannot be met well with public data: Windmill is the platform's own affiliate and names no analyst; Wright's pages are inconsistent; Quantace has an unexplained second SEBI number; Marcellus settled a SEBI insider-trading case (Rs 1.377 crore, 2020); seven others could not be verified. SEBI's own registry could not be queried by script, so every registration still needs a manual SEBI check.
4. At least 46% of thematic baskets found are no longer live; median life of closed ones is 2.0 years.
5. Momentum: a low-cost Direct momentum index fund is likelier to deliver than a paid smallcase; no paid momentum smallcase met basic transparency tests. India's trend regime is OFF (Nifty 50 about -14% year to date), and momentum index funds fell about 31% from the Sept 2024 peak.
6. Market backdrop (sector agent, Oct 8): Nifty 50 P/E 19.0 vs 10-year median 23.2; RBI hiked to 5.50% on Oct 7; Brent about $103; USD/INR about 96.6.
7. SIP: manual SIP was retired 4 May 2026. Automated SIP only at some brokers (Zerodha, SBI, HDFC, Axis, ICICI Securities, Kotak Neo; not Groww) and only where the basket minimum fits the SIP amount.

## Loss limit in rupees (a repeat of the worst fall seen on smallcase's own coarse series; true daily falls were deeper)
A 25% loss limit on Rs 2 lakh is Rs 50,000. Stake that keeps a repeat of the historical worst fall inside Rs 50,000: Auto/Banking (about -50%) about Rs 1 lakh; Infra (-39%) about Rs 1.3 lakh; IT (-31%) about Rs 1.6 lakh; Energy (-27%) about Rs 1.85 lakh. So the full Rs 2 lakh should not all go into one sector basket.

## Costs worked example (free basket, Zerodha charges, Rs 1 lakh held 24 months, rebalances applied)
About Rs 1,576 all-in (1.6%), including Rs 800 STCG on rebalance sales. A paid basket at Rs 8,400 a year adds Rs 16,800 over two years (65% of an assumed Rs 26,000 gain).

## Open questions (do not assume)
Broker; whether this money is separate from the existing portfolio; other realised equity gains this financial year (Rs 1.25 lakh LTCG exemption); whether free rule-based trackers are acceptable instead of a named manager; the actual holdings of any shortlisted basket (login-gated); a manual SEBI check of each registration number.
