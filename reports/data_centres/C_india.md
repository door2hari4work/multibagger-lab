## 0. Bottom line

1. **One START-SMALL, nothing cheap.** Techno Electric (TECHNOE) is the only name where valuation, balance sheet and a real data-centre option line up: 25.4x trailing / 16.2x forward P/E, EV/EBITDA 14.6x, net cash about 20% of market cap, no promoter pledge on screener, order book about Rs 11,000 cr (secondary). It still has real flaws (negative FY26 operating cash flow, 137 debtor days, Q1 profit down). Model-estimate 24-month expected return is only about +14% (Sec 6), so the cap is 1.0% of NW, staged.
2. **Everything with the best data-centre order books is priced for perfection.** Hitachi Energy India 111x trailing, Siemens Energy India 68x, ABB India 95x, CG Power 108x, Schneider Infra 148x, GE Vernova T&D 77x. Reverse-DCF (Sec 4) needs 35-60% EPS CAGR for five years just to earn 13% a year from a 20x exit. That is AVOID at today's prices, not because the businesses are weak.
3. **The "pure" data-centre operators are mostly unlisted or tiny.** Listed: Sify (US ADR, loss-making, illiquid), E2E (117x, GPU cloud), ESDS (listed 4 Sep 2026, 140x), Anant Raj (30x, mostly real estate). Unlisted: Nxtra, CtrlS, Yotta, STT GDC India, Sify Infinit (IPO on hold), AdaniConneX (JV). No data-centre REIT or InvIT is listed in India.
4. **Regime:** Nifty 500 22,019 vs 200d MA 22,869 on 2026-10-19 (-3.7%): filter OFF, verified. Use staged entry (thirds), smaller first tranche.
5. **Fit with existing AI exposure:** correlations to MSFT/GOOGL/AMZN/NVDA/TSM/MU are low in weekly data (0.0-0.4), but all of these names fall with the AI basket in stress weeks and carry Nifty 500 beta of 1.0-1.8 (Sec 7). Low correlation is not diversification of the shared drivers (rates, FII flows, oil, a hyperscaler capex cut). Keep the whole India data-centre sleeve to 2.0% NW.

## 4. What is already priced in (reverse-DCF)

## 5. Entry plans, caps, kill triggers

### 5.1 START-SMALL: Techno Electric (TECHNOE), cap 1.0% NW

What it is (FACT, secondary): EPC for power transmission/substations with an unexecuted order book of about Rs 11,000 cr (Aug 2026; Rs 9,600 cr at 30 Jun), targeting over Rs 4,000 cr EPC revenue in FY27 at 13-14% EBITDA margin, plus a data-centre arm (Chennai about 35-40 MW after optimisation; 150 MW "under discussion"; 250 MW by 2030; Rs 1,000 cr data-centre capex earmarked for FY27).
Why it might work in 1-2 years (INFERENCE): grid and substation capex for data-centre campuses (Tata Power cites Rs 15,000 cr Mumbai grid plan, 1,236 MW transmission-linked data-centre demand), a net-cash balance sheet that funds the data-centre build without leverage, and a multiple (16x forward, 14.6x EV/EBITDA) that sits below its own FY23-26 median and well below power-equipment peers (Hitachi 76x EV/EBITDA, ABB 61x).
Priced in: needs 13-18% EPS CAGR for 5 years to earn 13% a year (Sec 4); delivered 34% over FY23-26.
Three strongest bear points:
1. Cash conversion: FY26 OCF -Rs 590 cr and FCF yield -6.0%; receivables Rs 1,217 cr (137 debtor days, up from 108). FY25 OCF was +Rs 453 cr, so it is lumpy, but two of the last four years are negative.
2. Margins and earnings: Q1 FY27 consolidated EBITDA margin 15.8% (-178 bp) and PAT down 16% (press) / 31% (yfinance). The net cash earns treasury income that is part of profit (share unverified), so P/E flatters the operating business.
3. Data centre is still option value: no named anchor customer found, commissioning timeline and tenant mix unverified; Rs 1,000 cr capex spends about half the net cash.
Entry (thirds of the 1.0% cap, i.e. about 0.33% NW each):
- T1: now, in the Rs 900-960 zone (25x TTM / about 16x forward). Reason to wait is weak: the lab found timing carries no return edge, and regime OFF argues for the small first tranche only.
- T2: after the Q2 FY27 print (date not confirmed; check NSE board-meeting intimation) only if (a) debtor days not above 140, (b) consolidated EBITDA margin at least 15%, (c) order book at least Rs 10,000 cr.
- T3: when either Nifty 500 closes a month above its 200d MA (today 22,869) or TECHNOE closes above its 200d MA (about Rs 1,064) with the order book intact.
- Accumulate-on-what: add only on evidence (cash conversion, new data-centre tenant, order book), never on price falls alone. No averaging below the 25% kill line.
Trim/sell:
- Sell half at Rs 1,400 or higher (near the prior 52-week high of Rs 1,392; about +49%) or if trailing P/E exceeds 38x; sell the rest on thesis break.
- Concentration: if the position grows above 1.5% NW, trim back to 1.0%.
Kill triggers (observable): close below Rs 705 (-25% from Rs 940); H1 FY27 operating cash flow still negative with debtor days above 150; unexecuted order book below Rs 9,000 cr; consolidated EBITDA margin below 15% for two consecutive quarters; any promoter pledge above 0% or promoter stake (56.9%) below 50%; data-centre capex above Rs 1,000 cr without a disclosed anchor tenant (verify against filings).
Liquidity: ADV about Rs 17 cr; keep any single day's order under about 2% of ADV.
Worst case: 1.0% NW x 50% (observed 3y/5y max drawdown) = -0.50 pp of NW; at a -60% stress, -0.60 pp.

Sizing vs the investor's stated 20-25% loss limit on money put in (arithmetic from the lab sizing method: share of a new-money pool = limit / stress drawdown): with a 50% stress fall, at most 22.5% / 50% = 45% of any new-money pool earmarked for this sleeve can sit in such a stock. A sleeve holding several India data-centre names with 50-57% drawdowns (Anant Raj -57%, Techno -50%) shares that limit: do not exceed 45% invested in the sleeve; keep the rest in cash or index funds.

## 9. Data-centre-specific risks (India)

1. **Power and grid:** CBRE/Savills (secondary) call reliable power and land the binding constraint; Mumbai (over half of inventory) is grid-constrained, shifting builds to Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Visakhapatnam. Delays defer revenue for builders (Anant Raj, Techno) and orders for equipment names. A positive for grid-equipment orders, a negative for operators' timelines.
2. **Hyperscaler concentration:** Demand rests on a few buyers (Google US$15bn Visakhapatnam hub announced Oct 2025 with AdaniConneX and Airtel; Amazon US$12.7bn by 2030; per secondary reports). Colocation demand from enterprises is easing (Savills). Netweb top-5 customers about 81%; E2E has one Rs 1,000 cr contract.
3. **Capacity forecasts disagree:** CBRE about 1.7 GW end-2025 and over 3 GW by 2028; Cushman 1.5 GW colocation end-2025; others 2.2 GW. Add roughly 220-500 MW a year in 2026. Scope differences make overbuild hard to judge; I found no vacancy or rate data.
4. **Financing/debt:** Operators are capex-heavy (Sify net debt about 4x EBITDA; Tata Power 4.3x; JSW 6.4x; Adani Green 8.0x). Equity raises dilute (Anant Raj Rs 1,100 cr FY26, Netweb Rs 1,200 cr QIP, E2E repeated QIPs). Indian 10-year and US rates matter: the lab's macro report has the US 10y at 5.2% and a Fed hiking cycle.
5. **Lease cancellations / take-or-pay:** Most Indian disclosures are order books or MoUs, not take-or-pay. Hitachi's backlog and Netweb's order book are product orders; data-centre leases and framework awards can be cut. Not verified for any name.
6. **Obsolescence:** GPU clouds (E2E) carry 3-6 year useful-life risk against a 75% EBITDA margin that is before depreciation; air-cooled halls built now may need retrofits for liquid cooling (SPECULATION).
7. **Receivable and working-capital stress:** Techno 137 days, GVT&D 194, Netweb 112, Schneider 103; Q1 Tata Comm FCF negative.
8. **Input prices and FX:** Copper and aluminium drive cable revenue and margins (Polycab, KEI, Apar); the rupee is about 94.9 per US dollar; imported GPUs and equipment cost more.
9. **Governance/other:** Adani group entities carry a legal/governance overhang (status unverified); Anant Raj promoter stake 57.4% vs 60.1% a year ago (sources conflict: Emkay shows 60.12% at Mar-26).

## 10. What would make this thesis wrong

- A hyperscaler or AI lab cuts capex guidance, or a large AI financing fails (the lab's macro report flags this as the fastest break). India names fell 1-4% a week on average in past AI-worst weeks.
- Rates: the 10-year US stays at or above 5.2% and the Fed hikes again on 8-9 Dec; Indian equity multiples compress (Nifty 500 also stays below its 200d MA).
- Power-equipment backlogs convert slowly or HVDC awards slip; margins fall back as the equipment cycle matures.
- Techno specifically: negative OCF persists, debtor days rise, or data-centre capex is spent without a tenant.
- Colocation pricing weakens as the 2026 supply wave and IPO-funded builds arrive; enterprise demand shifts to cloud.
- A single large data-centre order proves to be a framework or MoU (as the lab found for Sterlite and Kirloskar Oil Engines).
- Copper falls sharply (June 2026 dealers postponed purchases): cable growth reverses.
